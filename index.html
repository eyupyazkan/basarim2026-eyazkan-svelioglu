Abstract

AbstractBAŞARIM 2026 · Poster 40

AbstractRemoving CO2 from methane is a key step in natural-gas purification, and adsorption on solid materials is one of the leading ways to do it. Two-dimensional MXenes (Mn+1XnTx) are among the most promising adsorbents: their composition can be tuned in tens of thousands of ways, yet only a handful have ever been measured. This work screens that space end to end on national high-performance computing (HPC) infrastructure, from automated structure generation and distributed high-throughput simulations to machine-learning (ML) surrogates that predict CO2/CH4 adsorption in seconds. A library of 20,592 hypothetical MXenes (six families; eleven transition metals; C or N; twelve surface terminations; two to five metal layers) was generated by one scripted workflow, geometry optimized and assigned DFT-derived charges, and an automated quality check kept 18,884 structures. Grand Canonical Monte Carlo (GCMC) simulations of an equimolar CO2/CH4 mixture at 308 K and 0.1, 1 and 10 bar (UFF/TraPPE force fields, Ewald electrostatics, RASPA 2.0), 56,652 independent runs in total, were distributed over TÜBİTAK ULAKBİM TRUBA accounts and laboratory workstations by MEM-CES Studio, an in-house workflow manager that prepares, submits, monitors and harvests every job. Six uptakes and fifteen derived metrics, including selectivity, working capacity, regenerability, and the adsorbent performance score (APS) for pressure- and vacuum-swing adsorption, were obtained for each structure. Across all families, more metal layers raise selectivity and lower capacity; –S, –Se, –Te, and –I terminations recur among the best performers; and at regenerabilities above 90 % the top MXenes reach APS values of 20–38.5 mol/kg, matching or exceeding the best of 3,816 MOFs screened in the literature. To stand in for further simulations, MX-Identifier, a rule-based fingerprint engine, reads each CIF, labels every atom as metal, X or surface group, builds the bond network and outputs a 97-descriptor fingerprint and a crystal graph. Four surrogates, Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, and a crystal graph convolutional neural network (CGCNN), share one two-stage hybrid scheme: a classifier first separates adsorbers from non-adsorbers, then a regressor trained on adsorbers only predicts the six uptakes. With 15,298 structures for training, 1,700 for testing and a held-out test set of 1,886 never seen by any model, the CGCNN reached 99.79 % classification accuracy and R² = 0.972–0.990 for the six uptakes on the held-out set. The deployed screening interface analyzes a structure at all three pressures in 1.18 s on a CPU, an estimated 37,000× faster than a single GCMC simulation. The HPC effort invested once is thus amortized through learned surrogates, and the resulting ecosystem (automated structure generation, distributed GCMC, crystal-aware fingerprinting, and hybrid ML) can be extended to other crystals and gas pairs.

1Motivation & Objective

Removing CO2 from methane is a key step in natural-gas purification, and adsorption on solid materials is one of the leading ways to do it. MXenes, two-dimensional materials with tens of thousands of possible compositions, are among the most promising adsorbents, yet only a handful have ever been measured. We screen this space end to end: automated structure generation, distributed GCMC simulations and machine-learning surrogates that predict adsorption in seconds.

New in this work: MX-Identifier, a rule-based fingerprint engine; one automated, distributed GCMC campaign over 18,884 MXenes; a classify-then-regress hybrid surrogate.

Read more from the thesis

MXenes are a family of two-dimensional early-transition-metal carbides and nitrides, named for their M–X bonding and their "-ene" sheet morphology (thesis p. 5). The screening space combines eleven metals, two core elements and twelve surface terminations (thesis p. 14, Table 2.1). Natural gas is typically treated by pressure- or vacuum-swing adsorption, and the adsorbent dominates the process economics (thesis p. 4). The central challenge is that the composition space is far too large to explore experimentally, which motivates a computational screen (thesis p. 4).

The library was generated from six MXene families reported in the literature using a Ti2CF2 unit cell as reference (thesis p. 14). Every template runs over 11 early transition metals (Ti, Ta, V, Y, Nb, Zr, Cr, Sc, W, Hf, Mo), two core elements (C, N) and twelve surface groups (thesis p. 14). A Perl workflow inside Materials Studio varied metal, C/N element and functional group in nested loops (thesis p. 16). 20,592 raw structures passed through a quality-control procedure that rejected structures whose terminations collapsed into the metal plane (1,042), whose cell angles deviated by more than ±3° after optimization (402), or whose simulation outputs were incomplete (264): 1,708 removed, 18,884 kept (thesis p. 16). The family distribution is given in thesis Table 2.1 (thesis p. 14).

Adsorption was computed with RASPA 2.0 using the UFF force field for the MXene framework and TraPPE for the gases (thesis p. 22). The CO2 model follows a TraPPE-like three-site rigid model (thesis p. 22). Every structure was simulated at three pressures, giving three CO2 and three CH4 uptakes, from which fifteen derived process metrics (selectivity, working capacity, regenerability, APS, AFM, SSP) are computed (thesis p. 36). Protocol validation: simulated CO2 isotherms for Ti3C2Tx and Mo2CTx lie inside the experimental range (poster Section 6; thesis p. 59).

5HPC Workflow Management: MEM-CES Studio

MEM-CES Studio runs the whole campaign from one application: it prepares the inputs, uploads and submits the jobs, monitors them, then downloads and parses the outputs and stores the structured results. Autopilot watches the pooled TRUBA accounts and the lab workstations and launches the next batch as soon as capacity becomes available.

Simulation handling: ≈ 15 min per structure by hand → ≈ 4,721 h avoided (est.)

Autopilot: monitor · collect · relaunch

Read more from the thesis

The controller is the second automation layer of the ecosystem; it runs every structure to all three pressures (poster Section 5). The distributed pool spans pooled TRUBA accounts — student accounts at 2 × 56 cores each and a project account at 4 × 56 cores — and the laboratory workstations. The thesis gives the campaign cost as an estimate, not a metered total: 12–18 core-hours per structure (thesis p. 86).

7MX-Identifier: MXene Fingerprint Engine

MX-Identifier is the core of this work: a rule-based, modular engine that extracts the fingerprint of every MXene. A model can only learn what its input describes, so the engine reads each CIF, labels every atom as metal (M), carbon or nitrogen (X) or surface group (T), builds the bond network and outputs a 97-descriptor fingerprint and a crystal graph. All four models start from this same description.

From the M.Sc. thesis

More analyses4 figures

1 / 4

Read more from the thesis

The thesis describes this step as the structure-to-descriptor pipeline of Section 3.2 (thesis pp. 28–35); the name MX-Identifier was given after the thesis. The 97 descriptors fall into six main categories (thesis pp. 28–29, Table 3.2). An atom-group identification algorithm classifies every atom of a structure as a transition metal (M), carbon/nitrogen (X) or functional group (T) automatically (thesis pp. 32–33, Figure 3.5). Bonds are detected from covalent radii, periodic in the sheet plane, and each edge of the crystal graph is an M–X or M–T bond (thesis pp. 30–31). The only input is the CIF: no manually provided quantity is required, and the same pipeline produces the 97 tabular descriptors and the crystal-graph representation fully automatically (thesis pp. 34–35).

Numbers in this section

  • 97 descriptors per structure reported · thesis p. 28, Table 3.2
  • six descriptor categories reported · thesis pp. 28–29
  • 4 models start from the same description (Random Forest · XGBoost · LightGBM · CGCNN).

8Hybrid ML Surrogates: Two-Stage Scheme

Every surrogate uses the same two-stage hybrid scheme: a classifier first separates adsorbers from non-adsorbers, then a regressor trained on adsorbers only predicts the six uptakes. Three tree-based models (Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM) read the 97-descriptor fingerprint and are compared with one another; a graph model (CGCNN)[6] reads the crystal graph and is compared against all three. Data split: 15,298 structures for training, 1,700 for testing and a held-out set of 1,886 that no model saw.

About 15 % of structures adsorb essentially nothing; feeding them to a regressor degrades it, so a gate classifier is applied first (thesis p. 34: 15.5 % non-adsorbers, CH4 < 0.01 mol/kg at 10 bar). The regressor predicts CO2 and CH4 uptakes at three pressures in one pass (thesis p. 36). CGCNN follows the original architecture of Xie and Grossman, with atoms as nodes and bonds as edges (thesis p. 33). The tree and boosting models use 97 descriptors (thesis p. 28). The data were split in three stages, stratified by family and adsorber label: a development set for training and testing and a held-out set of 1,886 structures that no model saw (thesis p. 27, Figure 3.1).

Models were evaluated on a stratified held-out test set of 1,886 structures never seen during training (thesis p. 25). The CGCNN classifier reaches 0.9979 accuracy; in the table, the CO2 and CH4 columns average the three pressures, and the overall 0.968 ± 0.008 averages all six targets (thesis p. 68, Table 4.4). Boosting (XGBoost, LightGBM) follows closely, showing the hybrid scheme — not a single algorithm — is the main driver of accuracy (thesis p. 68). The thesis Gradio tool labels atoms, builds the crystal graph and returns the prediction in about one second on a CPU (thesis p. 86, p. 91); its 1,886-structure held-out batch ran in 37 minutes on one 128-thread AMD EPYC 7763, which is where the 1.18 s per structure comes from. The two speed-up figures are estimates: one GCMC simulation's wall time, and the three simulations of one structure, each divided by the same 1.18 s analysis. The interface on this page replays the same models' results on 20 held-out structures; it is not the thesis tool.

10Conclusions: Integrated Screening Ecosystem

  • MXenes are among the most promising adsorbents: at high regenerability the top ones match or exceed the best MOFs[5].
  • An automated, distributed, crystal-aware ecosystem: MEM-CES Studio 3.0 distributes GCMC across computers, and MX-Identifier can extend to other crystals for screening and feature engineering.
  • From years to minutes: about 3 years of hands-on work removed; a 64-core CPU needs about 1.2 years for the 56,652 simulations, the surrogates about 6 min (est.).
  • Future directions: ML for structure generation and an end-to-end pipeline; transfer learning for other gas pairs with less data; generative AI to find the best MXene per gas pair in larger spaces.

Read more from the thesis

Future work includes relaxing each structure with machine-learned interatomic potentials and predicting the DFT partial charges with a second network trained on them (thesis p. 92). The library is not yet released; it is planned as an open resource (thesis p. 90).

Numbers in this section

  • about 1.2 years on a 64-core CPU for the 56,652 simulations estimate
