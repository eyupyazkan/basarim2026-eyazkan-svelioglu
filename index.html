60-second summary

Natural-gas sweetening removes CO2 from CH4 by pressure- or vacuum-swing adsorption, and the adsorbent sets the performance. Two-dimensional MXenes (Mn+1XnTx) can be tuned through the metal, the X element, the layer count and the surface group: tens of thousands of compositions, only a handful ever measured. We screened this space end to end. A scripted workflow generated 20,592 hypothetical MXenes; after relaxation, DFT charges and quality control, 18,884 remained. Each was run through grand-canonical Monte Carlo in RASPA 2.0 at 298 K and three pressures — 56,652 independent runs — driven by an in-house controller, MEM-CES Studio. A hybrid classifier–regressor surrogate, led by a crystal-graph network, reached 99.79 % classification accuracy and R² ≈ 0.97 on a held-out set of 1,886 structures. The deployed tool answers in about one second per structure on a CPU. HPC invested once, amortized forever.

1Motivation & objective

Natural-gas sweetening removes CO2 from CH4 by pressure- or vacuum-swing adsorption, and the adsorbent sets the performance. Two-dimensional MXenes (Mn+1XnTx) can be tuned through the metal M, the X element, the layer count n and the surface group Tx: tens of thousands of compositions, of which only a handful have ever been measured.

Objective: screen this space end to end with automated structure generation, distributed high-throughput GCMC and a hybrid graph-neural-network surrogate that makes every simulation instantly reusable.

20,592structures generated
18,884MXenes screened
56,652GCMC simulations

Read more from the thesis

MXenes are a family of two-dimensional early-transition-metal carbides and nitrides, named for their M–X bonding and their "-ene" sheet morphology (thesis p. 5). The screening space combines eleven metals, two core elements and twelve surface terminations (thesis p. 14, Table 2.1). Natural gas is typically treated by pressure- or vacuum-swing adsorption, and the adsorbent dominates the process economics (thesis p. 4). The central challenge is that the composition space is far too large to explore experimentally, which motivates a computational screen (thesis p. 4).

The library was generated from six MXene families reported in the literature using a Ti2CF2 unit cell as reference (thesis p. 14). Every template runs over 11 early transition metals (Ti, Ta, V, Y, Nb, Zr, Cr, Sc, W, Hf, Mo), two core elements (C, N) and twelve surface groups (thesis p. 14). A Perl workflow inside Materials Studio varied metal, C/N element and functional group in nested loops (thesis p. 16). 20,592 raw structures passed through a quality-control procedure that rejected structures whose terminations collapsed into the metal plane (1,042), whose cell angles deviated by more than ±3° after optimization (402), or whose simulation outputs were incomplete (264): 1,708 removed, 18,884 kept (thesis p. 16). The family distribution is given in thesis Table 2.1 (thesis p. 14).

3Automation I: scripted library generation

One scripted workflow built the thesis library in the Materials Studio GUI: it sweeps every M, X and Tx over each template, then relaxes, charges and exports. Since the thesis, the same workflow has run unattended on an extended library: 2,456 structures charged in 82 h.

Structure generation, lattice equilibration (Forcite, UFF) and atomic-charge assignment (DMol3 ESP charges, settings as in thesis Table 2.3) were automated in a single Perl workflow (thesis p. 16, p. 18). Since the thesis, the same workflow has run unattended on an extended library; a watchdog skips any structure that exceeds its time limit and restarts the session (run log, 29 Sep 2026).

Read more from the thesis

Adsorption was computed with RASPA 2.0 using the UFF force field for the MXene framework and TraPPE for the gases (thesis p. 22). The CO2 model follows a TraPPE-like three-site rigid model (thesis p. 22). Every structure was simulated at three pressures, giving three CO2 and three CH4 uptakes, from which fifteen derived process metrics (selectivity, working capacity, regenerability, APS, AFM, SSP) are computed (thesis p. 36). Protocol validation: simulated CO2 isotherms for Ti3C2Tx and Mo2CTx lie inside the experimental range (poster Section 6; thesis p. 59).

5Automation II: MEM-CES Studio

Our in-house application drives the whole GCMC campaign over pooled TRUBA accounts and the laboratory workstations: it prepares inputs, dispatches and tracks jobs, harvests and analyses results and launches the next batch. Every run is independent, so throughput scales with the cores allocated.

Pool: TRUBA student accounts, 2 × 56 cores each · project account, 4 × 56 cores

The controller is the second automation layer of the ecosystem; it runs every structure to all three pressures without manual resubmission (poster Section 5). The distributed pool spans pooled TRUBA accounts — student accounts at 2 × 56 cores each and a project account at 4 × 56 cores — and the laboratory workstations. The campaign cost is given on the poster as a thesis estimate, not a metered total: 12–18 core-hours per structure (thesis p. 86), or 0.23–0.34 M CPU-hours for all 18,884 structures (derived from that estimate).

6Screening results

  • Universal trend: more metal layers → higher selectivity, lower capacity
  • Capacity champions: thin SS2 / SSM2, PSA ΔN(CO2) of 2.0–2.5 mol/kg
  • Best balance: SSM3 and SSX3, selectivities approaching 100 at useful ΔN(CO2)
  • Record holder: vacancy-ordered Mo1.33C, ΔN(CO2) = 3.4 mol/kg with S = 262 (PSA), at R = 36 %
  • vs. 3,816 MOFs[5]: at R > 90 %, top MXenes reach APS 20–38.5 mol/kg; the best MOFs ≈ 20–30
  • Surface chemistry: –S, –Se, –Te and –I terminations recur among the leaders

7ML surrogates: hybrid two-stage scheme

Every model uses the same hybrid classification–regression scheme: a classifier first separates adsorbers from non-adsorbers; a regressor trained on adsorbers only predicts the six uptakes. Random Forest (bagging) is compared with XGBoost and LightGBM (boosting) on 97 descriptors, and all three with a crystal-graph network (CGCNN)[1] that reads the structure itself.

6uptakes + 15 derived metrics
97descriptors
4models (Random Forest · XGBoost · LightGBM · CGCNN)

Read more from the thesis

About 15 % of structures adsorb essentially nothing; feeding those zeros to a regressor degrades it, so a gate classifier is applied first (thesis p. 33, p. 36). The regressor predicts CO2 and CH4 uptakes at three pressures in one pass (thesis p. 36). CGCNN follows the original architecture of Xie and Grossman, with atoms as nodes and bonds as edges (thesis p. 33). The tree and boosting models use 97 descriptors.

Numbers in this section

  • 6 uptakes (3 pressures × 2 gases)
  • 97 descriptors

The deployed Gradio tool labels atoms, builds the crystal graph and returns the prediction in about one second on a CPU (thesis p. 86, p. 91). The 1,886-structure held-out batch ran in 37 minutes on one 128-thread AMD EPYC 7763 (poster Section 9). The "≈ 37,000×" compares per-structure surrogate time against direct GCMC on one CPU core, and the "> 280×" counts all 128 threads of the surrogate (NUMBERS §9); both use the thesis estimate of 12–18 h of single-core GCMC per structure as their base, so they are labeled (est.).

10Conclusions

  • HPC invested once, amortized forever: 56,652 GCMC runs become a surrogate that answers in about one second with R² ≈ 0.97.
  • Two automation layers made the scale possible: scripted structure generation and MEM-CES Studio campaign control.
  • Hybrid scheme: non-adsorbers are separated before any uptake is predicted, so every prediction stays physical.
  • Next: ML potentials and charge models to replace relaxation and DFT charges; an extended library is under way.
